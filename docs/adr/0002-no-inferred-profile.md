# The companion never infers a psychological profile

Calibrating how hard the companion presses is genuinely valuable, and attachment theory is the obvious frame for it. We considered having the companion infer the person's attachment style from how they behave. **We rejected inference.** The companion may notice a pattern in what the person *says*, offer it back tentatively, and remember only what they confirm.

## Why

Accuracy, not ethics, is the binding objection — the ethical worry is real but secondary, and would be arguable if the method worked.

It doesn't. Best-case LLM inference of personality from real interviews reaches **max r = 0.27** with **Cohen's κ < 0.10**, while test-retest **ICC > 0.85** — consistent *and* wrong, which is the worst available combination: a stable, confident, incorrect label that looks trustworthy precisely because it never wavers.

Attachment is also domain-differentiated (Sibley & Overall 2008; La Guardia et al. 2000, *JPSP*) — representations are organised hierarchically and a person can be secure with a parent and insecure with a partner. There is no single attachment style to find. And the only relationship joshua421 can observe is the one with *itself*, which is the one that does not matter: how someone relates to a piece of software predicts nothing about how they relate to God.

Self-report already does the job, better and cheaper. Grounding holds it in the person's own words — *"Direct. Plain and simple. Ask plainly whether I've actually been in the word, and press me gently when I'm drifting"* is more precise and more actionable than any four-quadrant label, and it cost one question to obtain.

This is an instance of a wider rule: joshua421 can observe **when** (a formative event is on the calendar) and it can receive **what** (the person discloses it). It never deduces what it was not told.

## The companion says what it is doing

Tact would permit an unannounced frame — asking well, and letting the answers do the work, the way a spiritual director does without announcing an assessment. We went further: **the companion is open about what it is asking and why.** *"I want to understand how you need to be met — can I ask you a couple of things?"*

The tempting alternative is a quiet reading, on the real methodological ground that knowing you are being read changes what you say. It is rejected because it inverts *they direct*: a conversation that quietly checks whether your self-report is *correct* has made the companion the authority on you while pretending not to be. The person names their own way of being met; the conversation surfaces and confirms it; it lands in their own `grounding.md`, in plain words, theirs to cross out.

The test, if a case is ever unclear: **if the person stopped and asked "what are you doing?", is the honest answer comfortable?** *"Trying to understand how you need to be met, so I don't get it wrong"* — say it any day. *"Classifying you"* — the discomfort is the signal.

## Consequences

Calibration is asked for, not deduced. Where the companion does notice something about the person, it offers it as a question rather than a finding — which `core/persona.ts` already requires under "discern, don't pronounce" — and only what the person confirms is written to Grounding.

The rejection is worth recording because inference is free, easy, and will be suggested again: the data is already there and the model will happily produce a label. The reason not to is that the label would be wrong in a way nothing would catch.
